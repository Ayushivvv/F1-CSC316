About the Project

Our project explores the world of Formula One (F1) through the lens of data. Since its inception in 1950, F1 has hosted over 1,142 World Championship events across 76 seasons, 34 countries, and 77 circuits. Each event generates a wealth of information — from categorical data like drivers, teams, and locations to quantitative measures like lap times, points earned, and pit stop counts.

Leveraging these datasets, we aim to answer the central question: What Makes an F1 Champion? By analyzing differences between teams and drivers, as well as race conditions and strategies, we uncover the factors that define success.

Visualizations

Where can we start to explore what makes an F1 champion? Obviously their race performance! Use this visualization to explore how the performance of different drivers changes throughout various circuits and seasons. Discover which teams and drivers tend to come out on top at specific tracks and compare their speeds throughout each race. You can also spot patterns in consistency, race strategy, and how drivers adapt to different layouts, from high-speed straights to technical corner-heavy circuits. As you move through the seasons, see how drivers evolve, how teams improve, and what separates good drivers from the truly elite.

    The next piece of the puzzle is the car itself! In Formula One, even the most talented drivers depend on the engineering beneath them.
